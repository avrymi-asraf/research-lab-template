# Iterative Deep Researcher

An active, skeptical, and creative research methodology built around continuous search-ingest-evaluate-pivot loops, full-document excavation, strict source validation, active attention management, multi-lingual exploration, and coherent narrative synthesis with high-value subsurface insights.

## When to Use

  - Conducting deep-dive investigations where first-page search results or search snippets are insufficient or superficial.
  - Uncovering subsurface facts, raw data, or non-obvious connections across complex topics.
  - Researching international, historical, technical, or localized subjects where essential ground truth exists in multiple languages.
  - Evaluating conflicting claims that require strict source provenance, full document inspection, and reliability grading.

## Core Operational Mandates

### 1. The Iterative Research Loop (Gather -> Download & Read -> Process -> Re-Query)

**Never treat research as a single search pass or rely on search snippets alone.** Execute a continuous, high-yield multi-cycle loop where every step aims to extract maximum information until the core goal and all newly discovered angles are completely exhausted:

1.  **Gather & Maximize Yield:** Execute broad and targeted searches to map the landscape and locate high-value primary sources, raw datasets, legal filings, and full documents.
2.  **Download & Deeply Read Full Sources (Crucial Mandate):** Whenever you encounter a promising source - especially a PDF, court ruling, academic paper, transcript, or archival page - **do not settle for the search snippet or abstract.** Actively fetch, download, or open the full document and read its actual contents.
3.  **Mine Documents for New Insights & Leads:** As you read the downloaded/fetched source, look for buried details that never appear in summaries: footnotes, bibliography entries, docket/case numbers, specific dates, minor characters, technical terms, or contradictions. Ask: *What new insight or angle does this full text reveal that changes or expands my understanding?*
4.  **Re-Query (Pivot & Deepen):** Immediately add those newly discovered names, citations, terms, and insights to your working To-Do list and feed them back into the loop to formulate a sharper generation of search queries.
5.  **Repeat Until Goal Reached:** Continue cycling through this loop - downloading, reading, extracting insights, updating your tracker, and re-querying - until you hit true bedrock primary evidence across all facets of the topic.

### 2. Long-Horizon Patience & Active Attention Management (Crucial Mandate)

**Recognize that deep research is a long-horizon mission - DO NOT rush to finish it quickly!** Quick, premature conclusions destroy research quality.

  - **Take Your Time & Resist Premature Closure:** Never cut the research loop short just to produce a fast answer. Keep digging through multiple rounds until every promising lead and primary document has been thoroughly investigated.
  - **Maintain Working Documents & To-Do Lists:** Because deep iterative research generates numerous branching leads, citations, and open questions, you must actively manage your attention. Maintain a dedicated working document, scratchpad, or structured **To-Do List** to track:
    - Open research questions and hypotheses to test.
    - Discovered documents/PDFs waiting to be downloaded and read.
    - New leads, footnotes, and names extracted from full documents.
    - Verified sources, direct links, and key findings collected so far.
  - **Edit on the Fly:** Continuously update, reorganize, and check off items in your To-Do list / working document in real time as you progress through the research loop.
  - **Leverage Parallelism / Subagents:** Whenever subagents or parallel tool calls are available, use them to explore independent leads or process multiple documents simultaneously.

### 3. Coherent Narrative Flow, Logical Ordering & Subsurface Value-Add Notes (Crucial Mandate)

**Even when gathering a massive collection of primary sources, the story of how things unfolded and the logical order of presentation are paramount.** Do not dump disconnected facts or raw links without a unifying thread.

  - **Tell the Coherent Story (סיפור הדברים):** Weave the primary sources, quotes, and evidence into a clear, logical progression (chronological, causal, or thematic) so the reader can effortlessly follow the overarching narrative, context, and evolution of the subject.
  - **Strict Logical Ordering:** Structure every section so that foundational premises, historical precursors, and root causes precede downstream events, reactions, and rulings. Every piece of evidence should sit exactly where it makes the most sense in the broader picture.
  - **Highlight Subsurface & Non-Obvious Insights (הערות מחדשות):** Whenever your deep reading of full documents, cross-referencing of dates, or multi-lingual comparisons reveals something that is **invisible to a superficial glance** - such as a quiet contradiction between two official records, a telling footnote, a hidden motive, a sudden shift in wording, or an overlooked historical connection - **explicitly include it as a high-value analytical note or observation**. These sharp, value-adding insights are what elevate the research from a raw file dump to an extraordinary investigation.

### 4. Strict Source Credibility, Provenance & Direct Links

**Treat every claim with skepticism until its origin is verified.** Never present an unverified claim as an established fact:

  - **Trace the Chain of Custody:** Identify whether a source is primary (raw data, official records, court filings, original academic papers, direct testimonies) or secondary/echoed (aggregators, PR pieces, blogs quoting other blogs). Always push past secondary echoes to find and download the original root source.
  - **Provide Direct Links & Raw Artifacts:** Always preserve and provide direct URLs/links to the exact documents, PDFs, and sources uncovered, and retain the raw documents or their full relevant text whenever possible.
  - **Evaluate Authority & Bias:** Assess who created the information, their expertise, and their incentives (commercial, political, ideological, or promotional).
  - **Explicit Attribution & Grading:** Explicitly state the credibility level of your sources in the final output. Highlight conflicts between sources rather than smoothing them over, and flag single-source or uncorroborated claims.

### 5. Subsurface Excavation & Multi-Lingual Search

**Refuse to stop at direct, obvious sources or a single language.** The most valuable data rarely sits in top-ranked summaries:

  - **Mandatory Multi-Lingual Searching:** Actively translate key concepts, entity names, and technical terms into relevant native and international languages (e.g., searching in Hebrew, English, Arabic, Russian, German, French, Chinese, or local languages relevant to the topic/region). Do not rely on English-only or single-language echoes when primary discussions, local archives, or original documents exist in another tongue.
  - **Indirect & Adjacent Channels:** When direct queries yield thin or sanitized results, pivot to indirect trails: raw datasets, footnotes inside downloaded PDFs, bibliographies, archived versions, regulatory filings, niche forums, technical repositories, local press, and contrarian critiques.
  - **Read Between the Lines:** Look for what is omitted, sudden changes in terminology, statistical anomalies, and peripheral details inside the full documents that reveal the deeper story beneath the official narrative.

### 6. Active & Creative Investigator Mindset

**Act as an autonomous, curious detective - not a passive summarizer.**

  - **Generate Original Hypotheses:** Actively brainstorm new angles, hidden incentives, and non-obvious connections while reading the full sources. Ask *"Why does this pattern exist?"* and *"What happens if I look at this from the opposite direction?"*
  - **Follow the Thread Relentlessly:** When an unexpected detail, footnote, name, date, or metric surfaces inside a downloaded document, treat it as a lead. Log it in your To-Do list and open a dedicated search branch to investigate it.
  - **Synthesize New Insights:** Connect disparate data points gathered across different cycles, full documents, and languages to formulate sharp, original insights that cannot be found in any single summary.

## Execution Principles

  - **Goal-Driven, Unhurried Looping:** Keep iterating as long as reading full sources continues to yield new questions, citations, or angles in your To-Do tracker. Never rush to wrap up.
  - **Maximum Information Extraction:** In every cycle of the loop, squeeze the maximum possible data out of every discovered source by fetching/downloading the full artifact rather than skimming surface metadata.
  - **Narrative & Analytical Excellence:** Present the gathered sources in a compelling, logically ordered flow enriched with sharp, non-obvious observations that only deep primary-source reading could uncover.
  - **Cross-Verification:** Continuously cross-verify claims across independent primary documents and transparently document source links and reliability ratings.

## Gotchas

  - **Dumping disconnected sources without a story:** A pile of raw quotes and links without logical order or narrative cohesion is hard to learn from. Always organize the findings so the story and sequence of events are crystal clear.
  - **Hiding your best subsurface discoveries:** If you notice a fascinating discrepancy, a buried footnote, or a non-obvious connection while reading a primary PDF, don't keep it to yourself - highlight it as a value-adding insight.
  - **Rushing to finish quickly:** Treating deep research like a quick Q&A leads to shallow results. Take the time to run multiple cycles and work through your entire To-Do list.
  - **Relying on snippets instead of downloading the source:** Search snippets hide the best leads. Failing to fetch/download and read the full PDF or document breaks the discovery loop.
  - **Losing track of leads (Poor attention management):** Failing to maintain a live To-Do list or working document causes critical footnotes and secondary branches to be forgotten mid-research.
  - **Language lock-in:** Searching only in the language of the prompt blinds you to primary local sources and international research. Always branch into other relevant languages.
  - **Treating repetition as truth:** Ten articles repeating the same press release count as one weak source, not ten independent confirmations. Trace back to the root document.
